# CellRX Indexation Remediation — Source Notes

These sources informed the August 2026 revisions to the priority IV stem-cell and chain-of-custody patient-education articles. They should remain available to the responsible clinical and compliance reviewers when evaluating future content changes.

1. **U.S. Food and Drug Administration. _Consumer Alert on Regenerative Medicine Products Including Stem Cells and Exosomes_.** The FDA explains that stem-cell products are regulated and notes that the only FDA-approved stem-cell products in the United States are blood-forming stem cells derived from umbilical cord blood for disorders of the blood-forming system; it further identifies multiple uses for which regenerative medicine products have not been approved. Available at: https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/consumers-biologics/consumer-alert-regenerative-medicine-products-including-stem-cells-and-exosomes

2. **U.S. Food and Drug Administration. _Important Patient and Consumer Information About Regenerative Medicine Therapies_.** The FDA advises consumers that unapproved regenerative medicine products may carry significant risks and discusses the importance of regulatory status and FDA oversight. Available at: https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/consumers-biologics/important-patient-and-consumer-information-about-regenerative-medicine-therapies

3. **Google Search Central. _Google Search Essentials_.** Google notes that technical eligibility does not guarantee indexing and recommends descriptive titles, headings, links, and careful handling of JavaScript. Available at: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials

4. **Google Search Central. _Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content_.** Google emphasizes clear sourcing, expertise, authorship, and stronger trust signals for health-related topics. Available at: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

## Editorial rule

All patient-facing health content should receive qualified clinical and compliance review before publication. Source links support reader context; they do not, by themselves, establish that a specific product is safe, effective, appropriate, or FDA-approved for a particular patient or use.
